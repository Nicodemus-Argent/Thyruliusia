## Geography

Fairmeadow lies in western Ladriande, approximately 14–16 miles from the coast and a similar distance from the Desert of Desolation.

The village occupies the center of a productive agricultural district comprising eight established farming hexes, including Fairmeadow itself. Each eight-mile hex encompasses approximately 41.6 square miles, giving the district roughly 333 square miles of land, although not every acre is cultivated.

The surrounding countryside contains fertile fields, pastures, orchards, and smaller farming communities. Nearby coastal grasslands provide excellent grazing, while fishing settlements along the water supplement the region's food supply.

Swamps and isolated patches of jungle occupy less hospitable territory closer to the waterways. These areas remain sparsely settled.

The nearby Desert of Desolation was formed by an ancient meteor impact. Although much of the desert is inhospitable, stretches of firm ground permit reliable overland travel.

The Hin are a rural halfling people whose traditions emphasize agriculture, home, family, and community life.

Humans and Hin have helped establish Fairmeadow's reputation as a comfortable and productive settlement. Its inhabitants are accustomed to the rhythms of planting, harvesting, livestock care, and seasonal trade.

The beastfolk population includes several ancestries, with their precise distribution yet to be established.

Fairmeadow is not a place defined by constant danger or political turmoil. Most residents are concerned with their families, livelihoods, and the continued prosperity of their community.

## Economy

### Agriculture

Agriculture forms the foundation of Fairmeadow's economy.
The most common crops include corn, beans, squash, and sweet potatoes. Their sweet potatoes are a much sought after export. Enjoyed locally with the home made butter.

Livestock Fodder: Fairmeadow maintains extensive grazing pastures and dedicated fields of alfalfa, Bermuda grass, forage sorghum, cowpeas, and pearl millet. Harvested hay and other stored forage sustain the village's cattle, sheep, and prized Meadow Stout workhorses when pasture is insufficient.

The surrounding farming district produces sufficient food to support the village and its neighboring settlements, with surplus goods entering Ladriande's internal trade network.

Farms vary in size, and individual agricultural hexes may contain smaller hamlets, farmsteads, and local gathering places.

### Glassmaking

Fairmeadow is also recognized as one of Ladriande's important glass-producing communities.

Its glassmakers primarily manufacture **glass bricks and blocks intended for export and subsequent shaping elsewhere**. These products are transported to other settlements, where specialist craftsmen can transform the raw glass into finished goods.

Fairmeadow does not specialize in precision optics or lens manufacture.

A smaller local tradition of blown glass produces distinctive wares associated with the village itself. The styles, colors, and customary designs of these pieces remain to be developed.

The silica used in glass production is obtained from deposits associated with the nearby desert, along the trade road to Hearthstone.

The glass made locally by the glass blowers adds a local wood ash to change the plain green glass to warm green yellow. The tiny bubbles and imperfections proof that they were made by hand and with intent for specific people.

### Fishing and Coastal Trade

Although Fairmeadow is not a port, nearby coastal settlements provide access to fish and other marine resources.

The western waters are difficult to navigate because of dangerous shoals and political disputes. Consequently, Fairmeadow relies principally on overland transportation rather than direct maritime trade.

## Roads and Trade

Fairmeadow is connected by road to Hearthstone to the southeast, which in turn connects with Briarlaize and the principal roads of Ladriande.

The route makes use of comparatively firm terrain, avoiding the less reliable sandy stretches and ridges of the Desert of Desolation.

Agricultural produce, livestock products, and glass bricks move along this road toward the nation's larger markets.

Despite its valuable exports, Fairmeadow remains a relatively quiet settlement, consistent with Ladriande's broader preference for limited foreign commerce.

## Character of the Village

Fairmeadow is a good place to live.

Its prosperity comes from productive land, dependable craftsmanship, and established relationships among neighboring communities.

Visitors encounter cultivated fields, well-kept farms, grazing animals, and the activity of glassmaking workshops. The village's distinctive glassware provides a point of local pride.

The settlement is neither a major military outpost nor a bustling commercial city. Its importance lies in the dependable contributions it makes to Ladriande's food supply and economy.


# Notable Locations

### The Stout & Saddle
Inn, stables, and wagon yard.
Proprietor: Borin the Tannhide

**Borin Tannhide — Hill Dwarf, Proprietor**

Borin spent his early and middle years producing fine leatherwork. He was an accomplished craftsman, but eventually grew weary of the tanning vats, harsh chemicals, and ever-present stench.

He sought work maintaining and repairing finished leather instead, finding employment at the Stout & Stable's stables. His expertise in saddles, harnesses, and tack made him invaluable to the establishment.

After several years, Borin took over the stables. Eventually, he inherited the adjoining inn as well.

He still enjoys caring for leather, but has no desire to return to the tanning pits.

**Merrick Vale — Human, Bartender**

Merrick is a charming, rakish fellow who loves conversation almost as much as he loves drinking.

Despite being a habitual drunkard and an accomplished shirker, he possesses an exceptional knowledge of wines and spirits—approaching that of a professional sommelier. He can discuss vintages, recognize quality, and recommend drinks with remarkable confidence and accuracy.

His expertise attracts customers who might otherwise pass through Fairmeadow without stopping. His easy manner keeps them entertained.

Merrick delegates sweeping, washing dishes, and other mundane work whenever possible. Borin tolerates this because Merrick is genuinely valuable to the business.
