# The Dragon Empire

## The Dragon Senate

The Dragon Empire is the oldest empire in Thyruliusia and, by almost every measure, the most advanced civilization in the known world. Its influence extends far beyond its political borders. The Empire established standards for calendars, weights and measures, coinage, law, and countless other institutions that have since been adopted by nations across the world.

The Empire is governed by a great assembly of Dragons, representing the many recognized dragon lineages: Chromatic, Metallic, Gem, and numerous others. No single lineage holds sufficient majority to dominate the Empire, and its government cannot fairly be characterized as either benevolent or tyrannical by the nature of its rulers. Its politics are the product of competing interests, negotiations, alliances, and the individual personalities of its sovereign members.

The Dragons are not simply rulers of their own territories. Many possess power and status extending far beyond their individual domains. Some are recognized as true Immortals, while others command influence through their own supernatural abilities, ancient alliances, and accumulated wealth.

### The Senate Hall

The Senate Hall is an immense underground cavern, regarded as the birthplace of the Dragon Empire.

Tradition holds that the first nine Dragons gathered here to debate the fate of the world and settle their own disputes. What began as a gathering of immensely powerful beings eventually became the foundation of an empire.

The original cavern has since been transformed into a magnificent legislative chamber. Magical illumination fills the enormous space, while great seating areas, desks, podiums, and other conveniences have been installed to accommodate the Empire's representatives. Its grandeur reflects the wealth and privilege of those who govern, rather than any need for practical austerity.

The chamber remains a place of considerable ceremony. When the Senate convenes, representatives from across the Empire gather here to debate matters of state, negotiate competing interests, and cast their votes.

### The Vox

As the Empire grew, the Dragons recognized that governing an empire of lesser, mortal servants required more attention than they wished to devote to it. A Dragon might spend years, decades, or even generations attending to its own interests, sleeping in its hoard, pursuing magical research, or tending to matters beyond the concerns of ordinary government.

The solution was the establishment of the Vox.

Each Dragon selects a trusted representative from among its supernatural servants and vassals. This individual becomes its Vox: its voice in the Senate and its representative in the affairs of the Empire.

The Vox are empowered through a supernatural connection to their patron Dragons. Their abilities are a manifestation of that relationship, often resembling the powers granted by a Warlock's patron. The connection also makes the status of a Vox readily apparent. Should a Dragon die or be deposed, its Vox loses the source of its authority.

When the Senate convenes, the Vox assemble in the Senate Hall. Each represents the interests of their patron, reports on the condition of their master's holdings, participates in debate, and votes accordingly.

Following the Senate's proceedings, the Vox return to their patron's domain and resume their responsibilities as its governor.

A Vox's appointment is not necessarily permanent. Death, voluntary or involuntary retirement, dismissal, or the Dragon's simple preference for another representative can end the appointment. Even an exemplary Vox may find themselves replaced by someone the Dragon considers better suited to the task.

The position is therefore one of immense prestige and considerable responsibility, but never one of guaranteed security.

### The Covenant

The Vox are only the most prominent members of a much larger collection of supernatural servants and vassals bound to individual Dragons. These retainers maintain the Dragon's interests, administer its holdings, enforce its will, and ensure that its influence persists across generations.

The supernatural relationship between a Dragon and its servants allows the Dragon to maintain authority over a domain without personally attending to its daily affairs. Some of these servants are powerful in their own right. Others are long-lived beings whose service may span centuries.

The Vox is selected from this greater body of servants, but the office does not necessarily belong to the most powerful or longest-serving member. Trust, judgment, and suitability for political representation are equally important.

### The Dragons and Their Domains

A Dragon's political domain does not necessarily correspond to the location of its lair.

Many of the Empire's most powerful Dragons maintain holdings and influence within the Empire while keeping their actual lairs far beyond its borders. Some reside in distant lands; others have established their homes on entirely different worlds or planes of existence.

Their servants and Vox administer their interests in Thyruliusia while their masters pursue affairs of their own.

This arrangement is one of the reasons the Empire can operate across such immense spans of time and space. Its political structure does not require its sovereigns to remain physically present.

### Immortality and the Outlands

Thyruliusia's connection to the Outlands has made it a place of unusual divine and supernatural significance.

The world is home to true Gods, numerous Demi-Gods of mortal origin, and beings who have earned or attained Immortality through their own actions and power. Immortality is not exclusive to the Dragons, nor is it necessarily bestowed by birth or lineage.

The Empire's rulers recognize that many beings of other races possess powers and status equal to, or even exceeding, their own. The term _mortal_ is therefore not a simple synonym for lesser being.

In the traditions of Thyruliusia, powerful individuals may eventually rise beyond the limitations of ordinary existence. At the highest levels of mortal achievement, the possibility of petitioning for Immortality becomes a recognized path.

The Dragon Empire exists within this wider world of divine powers, ascended beings, and ancient Immortals. Its rulers are among the most influential participants in that world, but they are not its only powers.
