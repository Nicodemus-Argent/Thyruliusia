Village Profile: Oakhaven-on-Mist (Thallogard)

- **Population:** Approximately **450 residents**. The community consists mostly of antlered cervine Green Elves, with a small, permanent district of traveling Hill Dwarves and Human river-merchants.
- **Geographic Placement:** Built directly over the rushing waters of the **Mistfen River**, just where the waters begin to flow northwest out of the deep valley.
- **Architecture:** The village does not touch the muddy riverbanks. Instead, the elves have braided giant, water-loving willow and iron-oak trees from both sides of the river together. The trunks cross over the water to form a massive, living bridge. Houses and walkways are carved directly into the high branches above the mist.

Core Resources & Economy

- **The River Transit Hub:** Because the river eventually flows all the way west into the neighboring Tanuki nation and the ocean, this village serves as Thallogard’s primary shipping port. Goods from the deep interior are brought here to be loaded onto flat-bottomed river rafts.
- **The Alchemical Tannery:** The elves use the unique, mineral-rich mud of the Mistfen River alongside specialized forest herbs to cure leather. This process creates exceptionally lightweight, water-resistant armor that is highly prized by rangers.

- **The Bulk Metal:** This village is where the "official" trade happens. The Hill Dwarves bring in heavy cartloads of mundane dwarven metals here. The Green Elves purchase it eagerly, using the public trade to convince outsiders that Thallogard relies entirely on imported metals.

Local Leadership & Tensions

- **The Leader:** **Elder Sylas Thorn-Crown**, a centuries-old cervine elf who negotiated the original river trade treaties with the neighboring kingdoms. He is fiercely protective of the river's purity.
- **The Current Tension:** A group of ambitious Human merchants from the west wants to build permanent stone docks on the riverbank to speed up loading times. Elder Sylas has flatly refused, as digging into the shoreline would damage the massive, ancient roots holding the living bridge-village together.
